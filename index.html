A Latent Dirichlet Allocation Analysis of Knowledge Tracing Research Trends
Authors: SeungRi Choo, Yun-Jung Choi* (*Corresponding Author)
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is a cornerstone of educational data mining, designed to monitor a learner's cognitive state and provide personalized feedback. With the recent surge in research driven by deep learning advancements, it has become essential to systematically categorize the existing literature and explore the intersection between technical innovation and psychometric value.

This study conducts a quantitative trend analysis of KT research using Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modeling. The dataset comprises approximately 1,000 core academic articles published in English between 2000 and the present, curated from the Web of Science (WoS) and ERIC databases.

To ensure data integrity, a rigorous purification process was employed, including the removal of duplicates across databases and the exclusion of literature outside the educational context. Following text preprocessing, the optimal number of topics is determined based on perplexity and coherence metrics. Furthermore, the study tracks shifts in research paradigms by analyzing changes in topic proportions across intervals of five years, specifically focusing on the transition from early Bayesian approaches to contemporary deep learning models.

The findings are expected to visualize the academic evolution of the KT field and provide foundational insights for future researchers seeking to balance computational performance with psychometric validity.
